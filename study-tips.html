Smart Study Tips for Students

Quizzes are not only a game. Testing yourself is one of the most effective ways to learn. Here are seven simple habits that work for school students and adults alike.

1. Test yourself instead of re-reading

Re-reading feels easy but you remember little. Answering a question forces your brain to find the answer. This is called active recall, and it is exactly what a quiz does.

2. Study a little every day

Twenty minutes every day beats three hours the night before an exam. Spacing your practice over days helps memory last longer.

3. Review your mistakes

Your wrong answers are the most useful part of studying. Write down or bookmark them and try them again after a day, then after a week. When you can answer correctly three times, you really know it.

4. Use the Pomodoro method

Study for 25 minutes, then rest for 5 minutes. After four rounds take a longer break. Short breaks keep your focus fresh.

5. Explain it in your own words

Try to teach a topic to a friend, a younger sibling or even to yourself. If you cannot explain it simply, you need to review it again.

6. Link new facts to things you know

Create small stories or pictures. For example, remember that Nepal has 7 provinces by linking them with the 7 days of the week.

7. Sleep and water matter

Sleep is when your brain stores what you learned. Students who sleep 7–9 hours remember much more than those who stay up all night.

Plan for a week

Monday–Friday: 20 minutes quiz practice + 10 minutes reviewing mistakes.
Saturday: take a Level Mode challenge and check the Smart Learning Report.
Sunday: rest, or challenge a friend to a Friend Challenge.
